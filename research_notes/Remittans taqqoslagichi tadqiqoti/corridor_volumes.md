# Inbound remittance volumes to Uzbekistan by sending country (corridor), 2023–2026

Research date: 22 Nov 2026. All figures USD. Unless noted, the original source is the Central Bank of Uzbekistan (CBU). Where a figure came only from a search-result summary and not from a fetched page, it is marked "(search summary, not fetched)".

Primary source used most: CBU, *Review of International Migration and Foreign Exchange Operations of Individuals*, March 2026 (English PDF, text extracted locally) — https://cbu.uz/upload/iblock/367/m44ddiv5ibi0qck9n5kadrpxofebimzi/2.-Sharh-migratsiya-ENG.pdf ; press release 17 Mar 2026 — https://cbu.uz/en/press_center/releases/3611322/

## Q1. Total cross-border transfers into Uzbekistan per year: 2023, 2024, 2025 and latest 2026

### Takeaway
Inflows were $11.4bn in 2023 (down 33% after the 2022 spike to $17.0bn), $14.8bn in 2024 (+31%) and a record $18.9bn in 2025 (+28%). In 2026 growth has slowed sharply: Jan–Sep 2026 reached $15.17bn (+8.9% y/y), and Q3 2026 grew only +2.9%. That points to roughly $20–21bn for full-year 2026 (my estimate, not a CBU figure).

### Inferences
- Implied Q2 2026 ≈ $9.3bn − $3.8bn ≈ $5.5bn. Implied 2023 Russia inflow ≈ $8.9bn ($11.5bn in 2024 ÷ 1.29).
- If Q4 2026 matches Q4 2025 (≈ $18.9bn − $13.93bn ≈ $5.0bn) or grows a few percent, full-year 2026 lands at about $20.2–20.6bn, only +7–9%. The 2024–25 boom (+30%/yr) has cooled to single digits. The slowdown is concentrated in Russia (see Q2 and Q3).
- The 2022 figure ($17.0bn) is inflated by the post-sanctions inflow of Russian money and relocants. 2022–23 is therefore a poor baseline. Use 2024–2025 as the base years.

### Gaps
- The CBU's own Jan 2024 release with the 2023 total was not retrieved. The 2023 figure ($11.4bn) comes from the CBU's 2026 retrospective chart.
- Monthly October 2026 data was not found.

## Q2. Breakdown by sending country: shares and growth rates

### Takeaway
Russia still dominates, but its share is falling: 87% in 2022, about 77–78% in 2023–2025, and 72.4% in Q1 2026. In 2025 the next corridors were Kazakhstan ($903m), the USA ($665m), South Korea ($612m), the EU ($563m), Turkey ($547m) and the UK ($188m). Each is only 1–5% of the total, but they grow faster than Russia. The UK (+39% in 2025, +62% in H1 2026) and the EU (+37%, +27%) are the fastest-growing large corridors. No CBU figures were found for Germany, Poland, the UAE, Israel or Japan separately.

### Inferences
- The six named non-Russia corridors sum to about $3.5bn in 2025 (18% of total). "Others" (remainder after Russia ≈ 78% and the named six) is roughly $0.7–1bn. It likely includes the UAE, Israel, Japan, Saudi Arabia, Canada and other countries, but the CBU does not break these out publicly.
- Implied 2025 Russia ≈ $18.9bn − $3.5bn (named six) − ~$0.7–1bn others ≈ $14.4–14.7bn (~76–78%), consistent with Kun.uz's "close to $15bn".
- Priority by absolute size: Russia (≈$14–15bn) is far ahead of any other corridor. Next come Kazakhstan, the USA, South Korea, the EU and Turkey (each about $0.5–0.9bn), then the UK (about $0.2bn, but fastest growing). If corridors are ranked by growth, the UK and EU rank highest.
- In H1 2026 non-Russia corridors grew 11–62% while the total grew 13%, so Russia must have grown only in the mid-to-high single digits. The Q3 2026 slowdown to +2.9% total suggests Russia flows are flat or declining in H2 2026. This matches lower Russian labour entries (Q3).

### Gaps
- No CBU per-country figures were found for Germany, Poland (only +9% growth in 2025), the UAE, Israel or Japan. The CBU publishes chart-level data for the "EU" only and lists selected member growth rates.
- No full-year 2025 or H1/9M 2026 Russia figure was published (only the Jan–Oct 2025 and Q1 2026 figures).
- No country breakdown was found for 9M 2026.
- 2023 UK figure and reliable 2023 Turkey/EU figures: the chart labels are ambiguous.

## Q3. Uzbek labour migrants by host country and trends

### Takeaway
Official Migration Agency figures (Aug 2026) count about 1.4m Uzbek labour migrants in nearly 40 countries: Russia 834,200 (~60%), Europe 258,000, Kazakhstan 84,400, Turkey 72,100, South Korea 46,400 and others 97,700. Russian patent and entry data give higher Russia numbers. These count entries and patents rather than a stock of people, so the series are not directly comparable. Russia entries are now falling (−13.3% in 9M 2026), while Europe (+42%) and East Asia (+26%) are rising.

### Cited Findings
- Aug 2026 (Migration Agency data cited in a CBU review): ~1.4m labour migrants in ~40 countries. Russia 834,200; Kazakhstan 84,400; Türkiye 72,100; South Korea 46,400; European countries 258,000; others 97,700. Women 398,900 (28.6%). The share in CIS countries fell 29%, Europe rose 42%, East Asia rose 26%. Uzbekistan has 48 labour-migration agreements with 23 countries, and 124,500 citizens were placed in jobs abroad in 2026 — [Gazeta.uz, 18 Aug 2026](https://www.gazeta.uz/en/2026/08/18/migration/)
- Earlier agency totals: >1.34m (Jan 2025, with ~600,000 in Russia) and ~1.2m (spring 2026). The methodologies differ, so these are not a clean trend — [Gazeta.uz, 18 Aug 2026](https://www.gazeta.uz/en/2026/08/18/migration/)
- Q1 2026: 1.34m Uzbek citizens in Russia on patents (−8.8% q/q, −1.8% y/y). Nearly 70,000 Turkish work permits (+14% y/y). Uzbek community in South Korea 99,600 (a broader count than "labour migrants") — [Kun.uz, 7 Jul 2026](https://kun.uz/en/news/2026/07/07/remittances-to-uzbekistan-rise-13-despite-falling-share-from-russia-7ffbe4)
- Russia: >1.535m labour entries by Uzbek citizens in 9M 2026, −13.3% y/y (TASS data), described as the first notable decline in 7 years. ~1.48m Russian work patents were issued to Uzbeks in 2025 vs 1.35m in 2024 (ICMPD). ~106,000 went via organised recruitment in 2025. All of this is from a search summary, not fetched — [RTVI](https://www.rtvi.com/news/uzbekistan-snova-pervyj-1-5-mln-grazhdan-respubliki-priehali-v-rossiyu-na-rabotu-za-devyat-mesyaczev/); [RTVI](https://rtvi.com/news/trudovye-migranty-iz-uzbekistana-rezhe-edut-v-rossiyu-gde-eshhe-oni-ishhut-rabotu/)
- 2025: the CBU says the number of Uzbek migrants in EU countries rose 13.2% and in Turkey 10.7% (search summary, not fetched) — [Spot.uz, 25 Mar 2026](https://www.spot.uz/ru/2026/03/25/organized-migration/)
- 2023 context: in H1 2023, Russian work permits/patents for Uzbeks fell 12% and migration registrations fell 43% to 1.7m — [Kun.uz, 23 Jan 2024](https://kun.uz/en/news/2024/01/23/volume-of-remittances-from-russia-decreased-by-almost-half-in-2023-central-bank)
- UK seasonal-worker visas to Uzbeks: 556 (2021), 4,228 (2022), 4,094 (2023) (search summary, not fetched; older data) — [Global Voices, Jan 2025](https://globalvoices.org/2025/01/18/uzbekistan-seeks-new-labor-markets/)
- Projected labour demand cited by the CBU/agency: South Korea short 1.2–1.5m workers, Japan 6–7m, Germany ≥400k skilled migrants per year, UK 90k care workers, UAE ~1m, Saudi Arabia 1.5–2m by 2030 — [Gazeta.uz, 18 Aug 2026](https://www.gazeta.uz/en/2026/08/18/migration/)
- IOM Uzbekistan publishes quarterly migration situation reports (latest seen covers Jul–Sep 2025) — [IOM](https://uzbekistan.iom.int/sites/g/files/tmzbdl2566/files/documents/2026-04/10-uzbekistan_migration_situation_report_july_september_2025_en_1.pdf) (not fetched)

### Inferences
- Remittance per migrant can't be compared reliably across corridors because the counts are inconsistent. On agency counts: Europe ≈ $2.9k per head ($751m UK+EU / 258k), Korea ≈ $13k ($612m / 46.4k) and Russia ≈ $11–18k ($14–15bn / 0.83–1.34m). The Korea ratio is unreliable: the 46.4k count likely excludes the wider 99.6k community. No US migrant count was found.
- The direction is clear: Russia is stagnating or declining in headcount (entries −13% in 2026), Europe is rising fast (+42%) and East Asia moderately (+26%). For a comparison product: Russia is the volume corridor; the UK, EU (Ireland, Lithuania, Croatia, Slovakia, Netherlands, Poland), the USA and South Korea are the growth and higher-value corridors.

### Gaps
- No reliable official counts of Uzbek migrants specifically in the UK, Germany, Poland, the USA, the UAE, Israel or Japan were found.
- The Russia stock (834k by agency vs 1.34m patents) is inconsistent between sources.

## Q4. Average transfer size, frequency and seasonality

### Takeaway
The CBU estimates the average annual amount per recipient via money transfer systems and bank transfers at $2,000–4,000. No CBU data on transaction counts or per-transaction averages was found. Seasonality is strong. Inflows are lowest in Jan–Feb (~$1.1–1.2bn/month), ramp up through spring, and peak in June–July (~$1.8–2.2bn/month) when seasonal migrants are working. Flows stay high through autumn (~$1.84–1.97bn in Jul–Oct 2025).

### Cited Findings
- "Preliminary estimates suggest that in Uzbekistan, the average annual transfer per recipient through conventional money transfer systems and bank transfers amounts to the equivalent of USD 2,000–4,000." The CBU also cites IFAD's typical $200–300/month and the World Bank's $500 benchmark — [CBU review, Mar 2026](https://cbu.uz/upload/iblock/367/m44ddiv5ibi0qck9n5kadrpxofebimzi/2.-Sharh-migratsiya-ENG.pdf)
- Monthly 2026: Jan $1.2bn, Feb $1.2bn, Mar $1.3bn, Apr $1.6bn, May $1.7bn, Jun $2.2bn. 2025: Jan and Feb $1.1bn each, Jun $1.8bn — [Kun.uz, 17 Aug 2026](https://kun.uz/en/news/2026/08/17/uk-eu-and-us-remittances-to-uzbekistan-surge-as-migration-geography-diversifies)
- Monthly 2025: Jul $1.97bn, Aug $1.9bn, Sep $1.84bn, Oct $1.86bn — [Kun.uz, 29 Jan 2026](https://kun.uz/en/news/2026/01/29/remittances-to-uzbekistan-jump-27-to-189bn-in-2025)
- Q3 2026 = $5.88bn (≈ $1.96bn/month) — [Gazeta.uz, 3 Nov 2026](https://www.gazeta.uz/ru/2026/11/03/money-transfers/)
- Cost context: the CBU says P2P transfers to Uzbekistan carry no separate fee, but the cost is embedded in the FX spread, which can reach 2–4% including intermediary bank fees. World Bank RPW (Q1 2025): the global average cost to send $500 is 4.26%. Cheapest G20 send markets: South Korea 3.07%, Saudi Arabia 3.14%, Australia 3.54%, USA 3.81%, UK 3.93%, Germany 4.10%, Japan 4.14% — [CBU review, Mar 2026](https://cbu.uz/upload/iblock/367/m44ddiv5ibi0qck9n5kadrpxofebimzi/2.-Sharh-migratsiya-ENG.pdf)

### Inferences
- H2 typically accounts for about 55–57% of annual inflows (2025: H1 $8.2bn vs H2 ≈ $10.7bn). A comparison product's traffic will likely peak from May to November, with a pre-New-Year bump unconfirmed.
- With $2–4k per recipient per year and a ~$500 benchmark transfer, frequency is plausibly 4–8 transfers per recipient per year. This is an inference, not CBU data.

### Gaps
- No CBU data was found on number of transactions, average ticket per transaction or per-corridor ticket size.
- Monthly data for Nov–Dec 2025 and Jul–Oct 2026 was not retrieved individually.
- World Bank RPW corridor-specific costs into Uzbekistan (e.g., Russia→UZ, Korea→UZ) were not retrieved here.

## Q5. Channels carrying the flows (MTO vs card-to-card vs bank)

### Takeaway
P2P card-to-card transfers are overtaking traditional money transfer systems. Card transfers went from 34% of inflows in 2023 to 40% in 2024, 46% in 2025 and 51.7% in H1 2026, when they became the majority channel for the first time. MTO volume stagnated (+0.3% in H1 2026). Bank/SWIFT is marginal (2% in 2025, 1.6% in H1 2026) and falling.

### Cited Findings
**Channel split, $m (CBU Diagram 9)** — [CBU review, Mar 2026](https://cbu.uz/upload/iblock/367/m44ddiv5ibi0qck9n5kadrpxofebimzi/2.-Sharh-migratsiya-ENG.pdf); [CBU press release](https://cbu.uz/en/press_center/releases/3611322/)

| Channel | 2024 | 2025 | 2025 share | Growth |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Traditional international money transfer systems (IMT) | 8,161 | 9,903 | 52% | +21% |
| P2P online to bank cards | 5,916 | 8,648 | 46% | ×1.4 (+46%) |
| Bank transfers (SWIFT) | 774 | 397 | 2% | −49% |
| Total | 14,851 | 18,948 | | |

### Inferences
- A comparison product must cover card-to-card / P2P apps (now the majority channel) alongside MTOs. The Russia corridor is likely where most P2P happens (Russian bank apps and Korona-type services to Uzcard/Humo), but the CBU does not publish a channel × country matrix.
- Sanctions on Russian clearing banks (Korona Pay's, July 2026) are a corridor-specific risk factor for Russia→UZ pricing and availability.
